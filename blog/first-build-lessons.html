What my first PC build taught me

I bought a 450W power supply for a system with a 300W GPU, an overclockable CPU, and three drives, because the number "450" sounded like plenty at the time. It was not plenty. The store I bought it from wouldn't take it back once the anti-static bag was open, so lesson one cost me about $60 and a weekend.

Lesson two was patience. I routed every cable exactly once, snapped a dozen photos of my old rat's nest on the way, and then found a 30-second video that showed the actual trick I'd been missing: build the loom outside the case first, then drop it in. An hour of prep saved three hours of fighting a side panel that wouldn't close.

None of that compares to the first boot, though. Screen came up on the first try, no beep codes, no troubleshooting forum required. I still don't fully understand why that moment felt as good as it did — I'd only assembled parts other people designed — but it did, and it's the reason I've built every machine since instead of buying one.

A few photos of that build, and the ones after it, are over in the photos section.
