Why I switched my desktop to Omarchy

I spent most of last year doing the thing every Linux user does at least once: distro hopping. Fedora for a few months, then Arch by hand, then back to Ubuntu when a driver update broke my monitor setup for the third time. Every install felt like a part-time job I hadn't applied for.

Omarchy is the first setup that got out of my way. It's Arch and Hyprland underneath, but the defaults are opinionated in a way that actually matches how I work: sane keybinds out of the box, a theme system that reaches every app at once instead of just the terminal, and none of the multi-hour ricing sessions I used to lose whole weekends to.

What actually changed

The short version: I spend my time using my computer again instead of configuring it. Switching themes system-wide takes one keybind, which is the entire reason this site has a theme switcher in the top bar — I wanted the same feeling here.

I still tinker. I just do it on purpose now, not because something broke.
